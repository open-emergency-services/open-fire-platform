# 🔥 Open Fire Platform  *(working name)*

## Firefighters have to buy software just to do their job. A few investors bought that software — and the price is exploding.

Free, open-source software for fire departments. Yours to run, yours to keep, and free — the way a public service should be.

---

**What it is** — a single, open platform for the software a fire department needs to operate: records, federal reporting, EMS, scheduling, apparatus, training. Free to use. Free to self-host. No lock-in.

**What it does** *(being built in the open, one piece at a time):*

- **Incidents** — logs every call and files the mandatory federal report (NERIS).
- **EMS / patient care** — records treatment on the ambulance (NEMSIS).
- **Scheduling** — who's on shift, who's on call.
- **Apparatus & gear** — tracks trucks, SCBA, and turnout-gear checks.
- **Training** — logs hours and certifications.
- *…more to come.*

> **Status: early.** Being built in the open, starting with the free federal reporting piece. Not finished — but real, and yours.

---

## Why does this exist?

Every fire department in America is **required** to report to the government — every fire, every medical call. To do that, and to run day to day, they buy software. For a long time, it was cheap.

Then private-equity firms started buying the companies that make it — and buying up the competition and shutting the cheap options down. Today, an estimated **two-thirds of U.S. fire departments** run software from **one** private-equity-backed company. With the competition gone, the prices climbed:

- A Connecticut volunteer department — total budget **$132,000 a year** — was paying **$795 a year** for its software, until the vendor was bought up and the replacement was offered at **$5,000**.
- A Maine department faced **~$2,500 → $15,000 over five years**; a neighbor saw a **110% increase in two years**, with **"13 years of all of our training records, all of our incident reporting"** locked inside one vendor's system.
- It got bad enough that in **January 2026, two U.S. senators asked the DOJ and FTC to investigate.**

Most fire departments are **volunteer**, funded by the small towns they protect. A software bill that leaps by thousands of dollars comes straight out of the community. **Saving your life shouldn't come with a private-equity markup.**

## It doesn't have to be this way

The mandatory federal reporting system is **already free**. And some states — like **North Carolina** — already buy solid software and give it to all their departments at **no cost**. It's proof this can simply be a public service.

This project is that public service, built in the open: **free software any department can run — that nobody can buy up, lock down, or hold your data hostage with.**

- ✅ Free and open source, licensed ([AGPLv3](LICENSE)) *specifically so no one can take it, close it, and sell it back to you*
- ✅ Self-host it — your data stays yours, exportable anytime
- ✅ No vendor, no lock-in, ever
